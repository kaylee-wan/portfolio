Houses, Drawn from Photographs

Procreate iPad Architectural Illustration Linework
Summary

My first attempt at drawing on an iPad in Procreate. Both pieces are drawn from real photographs — a Victorian block in San Francisco in line and grayscale, and a modern house in Seattle rendered in full color — drawn as a birthday gift for a married couple — the two houses they've lived in, one in each city. I printed both drawings, framed them, and mailed them over — something to keep.

The Drawings
01 · Victorian Row, San Francisco

A San Francisco block in line only, with grayscale reserved for shadow. Repeating bay windows and stair rails down a receding street meant the perspective had to hold across a dozen repeated shapes — no faking it.

02 · Modern House, Seattle

Cedar soffit, metal siding, and glass garage — each material gets its own mark-making so the surfaces read differently even at a glance. The hard cast shadow across the facade was the piece that made the whole drawing sit in real sunlight.

What I Learned

The first time I opened Procreate I had no idea what I was doing. I couldn't control the line — I'd draw the same edge over and over, undo it, draw it again, and still not be happy with it. It felt like my hand and my eye were working against each other. That went on for a while. It only got better through repetition: more lines, more hours, and eventually a little confidence. Turns out the boring advice is true — you get it by doing it a lot.
